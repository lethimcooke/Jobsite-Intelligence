# Second business on the Asterfield engine: brainstorm, scoring and pick

Prepared October 13, 2026 from `Playbook.md` and the `reference/` pack. Volumes marked
**measured** were queried live today from the public datasets named; everything else is marked
**estimate** or **unverified**.

**Bottom line.** The best fit is **TCEQ construction stormwater Notices of Intent (NOIs) sold to
roll-off dumpster and portable toilet haulers**. The record is free, refreshed daily and
statewide. It names the operating business and the site, and about 745 new sites a month show up
in Texas (measured, September 2026). The buyers are small, owner-run, and already pay for web
leads. Neither the buyer nor the dataset overlaps ASI. Its weak spot: haulers leave no permit
trail, so the "your own recent job" line that tripled ASI's replies is mostly unavailable. The
bigger issue is that ASI has 0 paid customers. A second business on the same offer inherits
whatever is stopping that first sale. Section 5 covers both.

Reasons behind the scores that decide the ranking:
- **#1 easy sale = 4:** haulers already buy web leads (home-services leads average about $91; the
  closest dumpster proxy, "Storage," runs about $120 per lead, per the LocaliQ figures quoted by
  WebTonic). That makes $500 for 15–20 commercial sites, each worth many hauls, a familiar kind
  of purchase.
- **#1 timing = 3, not 4:** TCEQ coverage starts *immediately* on an electronic NOI, and the NOI
  is filed before the site discharges stormwater. The record therefore arrives at about
  mobilization. Toilets are usually picked then. Most roll-off volume comes later, in the
  vertical and finish phases.
- **#3 RRC fails criterion 8:** operators buy through master service agreements and approved
  vendor lists, small vendors don't habitually buy leads, and the permit data is already
  repackaged (Enverus, Apify feeds at about $10 per 1,000 permits).
- **#11 and #13 score 1 on saturation:** new-NPI lists sell for $9 a state a week (UpsideRadar on
  Apify), and new trucking authorities are notoriously flooded with insurance calls. Both also
  score 2 on clean claims, because many "organizations" are one person's practice or truck.
- **#8 child care fails timing:** an "Initial" permit is issued when the center is already open.
- **#9 WARN fails trigger:** the data.texas.gov copy (`8w53-c4f6`) hasn't been updated since
  June 23, 2026 (measured), and closures are a small share of notices.

### 4b. First email (S1)

The ASI formula: one filed site, a fit line labeled as our read, the standard price line word
for word, one yes/no question, the full signature, and the opt-out footer. The facts below come
from today's record; re-check them, and run the cross-business conflict check, before any real
send.

```
Subject: Westlake Court Business Center, Bee Cave Road

Hi [Company] team,

TCEQ's Central Registry lists a construction stormwater authorization (TXR1526ZS) dated October 12 for Westlake Court Business Center at 910 Beaver Trail and 3663 Bee Cave Road in Travis County. The operator on file is Capital Constructors Group LLC. It's about [9] miles from your [Oak Hill] yard, and a commercial site like this is the kind of job your 20- and 30-yard roll-offs serve; that's our read, not the filing.

We're a research service with no connection to the project: each week we pull new construction sites from state records and match them to haulers by service area. The sample costs nothing; after it, our 30-day pilot is a one-time $500 for 15–20 verified projects, with no subscription.

Want the free five-site sample?

Austin Jobsite Intelligence
Asterfield Group
sites@asterfieldgroup.com

If this isn't useful, reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.
```

Tier A in this market: a hauler's own published job or customer type ("the commercial and
roofing roll-offs on your site"), plus the yard distance. Tier B: service area and container
sizes from the hauler's own site. As in ASI, never invent a past job.

### 4c. Banned claims for this market

Add these to `Validator_Rules.json` `banned_phrases` for this business, alongside ASI's
existing list (construction started, unselected, open for bid, call phrases):

1. **Start of work:** "has broken ground", "broke ground", "groundbreaking", "site is active",
   "mobilized", "clearing has begun", "work has begun". An NOI is an authorization, not proof
   of activity.
2. **Approval language:** "TCEQ approved", "approved the project", "permitted the project".
   Coverage under a general permit isn't a project approval; say "lists an authorization."
3. **Vendor status:** "no hauler", "hasn't chosen", "not yet selected", "accepting quotes",
   "taking bids", "open for bid".
4. **Need as fact:** "needs a dumpster", "needs portable toilets", "will need". Always "our read."
5. **Relationship:** "on behalf of", "referred by", "working with the", "we represent", "the GC
   asked". We sell research, not introductions.
6. **Size and value:** acreage, project cost or haul counts not stated in a cited record (the
   Central Registry rows carry no acreage). No "5+ acres" unless the record says it.
7. **Compliance or regulatory advice:** "out of compliance", "violation", "required to use",
   "you must", anything about SWPPP adequacy, landfill rules or disposal law.
8. **Exclusivity and urgency:** "exclusive", "before anyone else", "first to know", "guaranteed".
9. **People:** no person's name from the NOI contact fields or principal names. Skip any site
   whose operator is listed as an individual or sole proprietorship, or whose principal name is
   a person's name.
10. **Homebuyers:** never mention lot buyers, residents or future homeowners of a subdivision.

## 5. Biggest risks, stated plainly

**#1 NOI → haulers: the 13% lever may not exist here.** ASI's replies tripled when the email named
the shop's own recent job, and that came from sign permits. Haulers file no permits, so most
emails will be Tier B, which is ASI's weaker tier. Second: the record arrives at about
mobilization. Toilets are often already placed, and national builders (Pulte, Meritage, Taylor
Morrison) and large GCs run national hauler accounts. The open money is local commercial GCs in
the vertical phase, and fewer of the 745 sites a month fit that.

**#2 NOI → erosion control: the SWPPP firm is usually already hired.** The operator certifies a
SWPPP before filing the NOI, so the person who wrote it often installs or inspects the BMPs. The
real opening is final stabilization months later, which makes today's NOI feel early, and the
sample would have to explain a delay. It would also email the same sites as #1, so run one, not
both.

**#11 NPPES → billing and credentialing: it's already a $9 list.** Anyone can buy Texas's weekly
new-organization NPIs for $9, which undercuts a $500 pilot. Many "organizations" are one
counselor or dentist, so this edges toward the "no individuals" line. I'd drop it.

**All three: ASI has 0 paid customers.** That's the biggest risk, and it isn't specific to the
new market. Thirteen replies and several samples (Hardman, Alamo, ASAP, Texas Five Star, Premier,
Capital Shade) have not produced a $500 payment. The tracker's own checkpoint says that if the
first 10 tailored samples produce no sale, revisit the offer rather than adding volume. A second
business with the same offer (free sample, then $500 for 30 days) adds volume. If the blocker is
the offer itself (price, a research product without a guaranteed result, or the gap between
"interesting sample" and "pay"), the new business will hit the same wall. It will also split the
time of the one person who sends every email.

Two existing rules collide with launching now:
- **Sending:** the tracker says "no new sending domains until the first paying client," with a
  cap of 60 total sends a day from the one mailbox. A new business either shares that cap with
  ASI or waits.
- **Overlap:** the same site can show up in both businesses (Kyle Park today). Add a
  cross-business check on RN and address to the validator before the first send.

**Recommendation.** Don't launch the second business yet. Do two cheap things now:
1. Build the NOI pull (one Socrata query per region, the same style as `pull_signals.js`) and keep
   a stockpile. It costs little and is ready when needed.
2. Put the effort into ASI's first sale. If ASI gets no sale by your checkpoint, test a variant of
   the offer on haulers as one controlled change (for example a smaller first step), using a
   batch of 20 S1s inside the existing 60-a-day cap. Then you learn about the offer and the new
   market at the same time, without a new domain.

## Addendum, October 14: dropping the Texas-only and match-ASI constraints

With those constraints relaxed, one idea beats the stormwater/hauler pick on the criterion that
matters most while ASI has 0 paid customers, which is criterion 8 (easy first sale):

**New clinic NPIs → medical and dental office cleaning companies.**
- **Trigger:** the CMS NPPES weekly file, newly enumerated Type 2 NPIs. Filter to a commercial
  suite address, clinic-type taxonomies (urgent care, imaging, dental, physical therapy,
  behavioral clinics) and organization names with no person's name or credential. **Measured,
  Oct 5–11, 2026:** 292 new Texas organization NPIs, of which 64 pass the filter; 3,106 US, of
  which 643 pass. That's about 275 a month in Texas and 2,700 a month nationally. Some survivors
  are management or telehealth LLCs in shared suites, so a currency check is still needed
  (two LLCs on 4100 Alpha Rd Ste 445, Dallas, in that week's file, for example).
- **Buyer:** 6,072 Texas janitorial sales-tax permit holders (NAICS 561720, **measured**), and the
  idea is national. Cleaners already pay for leads: janitorial appointment setting runs about
  $50 a set appointment at the low end and $200–500 a qualified meeting from agencies. That
  makes $500 for 15–20 new clinics a familiar purchase.
- **Saturation:** the $9 weekly NPI lists are sold to billing and credentialing firms, not to
  cleaners. I found no one selling new-clinic leads to janitorial companies.
- **Ticket:** dental janitorial runs $200–800 a month (one source), about $2.4k–9.6k a year, more
  for urgent care and imaging. One account clears 10x the pilot over a multi-year contract, not
  in the first year for a small office. The ticket is thinner than the haulers'.
- **Risks:** the vendor's own job usually isn't public (Tier B fit lines from the cleaner's own
  site); some new NPIs are re-registrations at existing locations; a person's name sometimes sits
  in the organization name and must be filtered; the same clinic could be an ASI sign project
  (different buyer, but add a cross-check). The reply rate is unknown, though ASI's medical hooks
  replied at 20%.

Revised view at the time: clinics → cleaners first, stormwater NOIs → haulers second. **Superseded
by Addendum 2**: the record doesn't imply cleaning, and cleaners are among the most heavily
pitched buyers, so this fails the nicheness test.

## Addendum 2, October 14: weighted re-ranking on the factors that matter most

### How the factors are weighted

The hard gates from Playbook section 5 still apply (criteria 1, 2, 4 and 8 must score 3 or
more). Above the gates, each factor is weighted by what ASI's own results say:

| Factor | Weight | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Easy first sale (the buyer already pays for leads) | ×3 | ASI has 13 replies and 0 paid; this is the gap. |
| The record implies the vendor's specific product (niche) | ×2 | It's why "new storefront → sign" works, and why "new clinic → cleaning" doesn't. |
| The vendor's own past job is in a public record (Tier A) | ×2 | It took ASI's replies from 4% to 13%. |
| Low saturation | ×2 | Flooded buyers don't reply. |
| Ticket, trigger quality, fragmentation, timing | ×1 each | Covered by the gates; these break ties. |

Maximum is 65.

### What was newly researched (October 14)

- **Restaurant pest violations → commercial pest control** (record implies product: "evidence of
  mice" means pest control). NYC's open inspection data shows **928 restaurants with pest-related
  violations in September 2026** (measured, codes 04K/04L/04M/04N/08A). Chicago shows 222
  inspections citing pests (measured). Against it: an Apify actor already sells exactly this feed
  (NYC, Chicago, Boston, Austin, King County), and a prospecting guide teaches the same pitch.
  A restaurant pest contract runs about $660–1,656 a year (Lloyd Pest's published ranges), so one
  account is not 10x the pilot. Most restaurants already have a pest vendor, and the email would
  imply the incumbent failed. Texas has no current violation-level open data (Dallas's dataset
  stops in January 2024).
- **City energy-audit and benchmarking mandates → energy auditors** (record implies product).
  Fails the trigger gate: the data is annual (Chicago last updated Feb 2025, NYC LL87 in 2021),
  and NYC LL97 consulting is crowded.
- **New trucking authorities → DOT drug and alcohol testing consortiums** (record implies product:
  owner-operators must join a consortium). Fails on ticket: enrollment runs about $149 a year

- **TTB permits → craft-beverage vendors.** Fails the trigger gate: TTB does not publish brewer

### Conclusion

**Stormwater NOIs → roll-off and portable toilet haulers stays the pick.** It is the only idea
that scores 4 or better on all three of easy sale, nicheness and low saturation. Its known gap is
Tier A personalization (score 2). No public record names haulers at job sites, so every email is
Tier B. That makes the test simple: if Tier B emails to haulers can't reach ASI's 7%, the idea
fails fast and cheaply.

What would make it stronger:
1. Send only commercial, industrial and institutional sites (about 250–300 a month statewide).
   Skip subdivisions run by national builders, utility lines and roads.
2. Replace the "your own job" line with two verifiable specifics from the hauler's own site:
   the distance from its yard, and the container sizes or commercial services it publishes.
3. Run one batch of 20 S1s inside the existing 60-a-day cap before building anything else.
