# Jobsite Intelligence: outreach copy v2

Draft, October 14, 2026. Replaces `docs/Outreach_Copy_v1.md` for new drafts. Fixed terms don't
change: free five-site sample, then a one-time $500 30-day pilot for 15–20 verified projects,
with no subscription. No calls. No em dashes. No person's name anywhere. The S1 price line is
`ops/rules.json` `standard_price_line`, word for word, once.

## What changed from v1, and why

1. **Same formula, shorter and split.** ASI's formula stays: one filed fact, one fit line
   labeled "that's our read, not the filing", one yes/no question. Nothing in the research gave a
   sourced reason to change it. What changes is length and layout: the filed fact and our read
   now sit in separate paragraphs, so the reader sees what the record says apart from what we
   think, and the "about us" line drops from 25 words to 10 (the record in the first paragraph
   already shows how we work). The 19 Houston bodies went from 116–127 words to about 100–110. Elite cold campaigns average under 80 words on the first touch (Instantly 2026
   benchmark), and Hunter's 34-million-email study found the best reply rate at 20–39 words, but
   with a spread of only 3.4–4.5% across lengths. So length helps a little; it isn't the main
   lever. We can't reach 80 words while the fixed 25-word price line is in every S1; that's the
   user's call, not a copy change.
2. **The question ties the sample to the hauler's yard.** "Want the free five-site sample?"
   becomes "Want five sites like this near your yard, free?" It's still one yes/no interest
   question (Gong Labs' 304k-email study, as reported secondhand: interest questions beat
   meeting asks on a first touch; Instantly: one binary question). It now names what the hauler
   gets in its own terms: sites, near its yard.
3. **Hauler words, not ours.** Fourteen Houston hauler sites (list below) talk about "job
   sites", "contractors", "commercial builds", "new construction", swaps and same-day delivery.
   None of them say "stormwater" or "authorization". The fit line now says "a commercial job
   like this" instead of "a commercial site like this", and the question says "near your yard"
   instead of "by service area". The record itself is still named exactly, because that is
   what makes it checkable.
4. **S2 adds a new angle: who arranges site services.** In v1, S2 restated the same record. Now
   it points to the operator on file and its main office line, the "first call" column in the
   sample. Construction waste guidance puts waste services under one project authority, usually
   the GC or construction manager (City of Lincoln C&D recycling fact sheet), and treats debris
   logistics as a preconstruction decision (Construction Review). Most of our operators on file
   are general contractors (ICI Construction, Burton Construction, FCL Builders, Clay
   Development & Construction, Cannon Construction). Instantly reports that follow-ups that read
   like a reply and add a new angle do about 30% better than formal bumps. The S2 never says
   whether a hauler has been picked; it says who to ask.
5. **Subject lines get shorter.** Woodpecker's 2025 benchmark (first emails, about 1,200
   companies) shows the best reply rates at 20–29 characters and 3–4 words, falling past 40
   characters. v1 subjects ran 30–75 characters ("Teadit North America Inc. Manufacturing
   Facility, 10545 Red Bluff Rd"). v2 uses the site name alone, plus the street only when the
   name is under 10 characters ("BMC, 7355 West Rd").
6. **One A/B test for the first 20 sends: the subject line** (below). It's the cheapest change
   to test, it doesn't touch the formula, and the benchmark data points at it.
7. **E1 and E3 fixes.** v1's E1 and E3 greeted "[first name or Company team]", which breaks the
   no-names rule; both now greet "Hi [Company] team". v1's E3 said "a single site that runs
   roll-offs for a few months covers it many times over", which is a job-size claim no record
   supports. v2 leaves the math to the hauler, who knows its own rates.
8. **New: E2 and objection replies.** E2 is the nudge after a sample goes out with no answer.
   The objection replies cover the questions haulers are most likely to ask, each answered
   from the record or labeled as our read.
9. **Mailing address.** Pending from the user. Every v2 cold template (S1, S2, S5) carries a
   `[MAILING ADDRESS]` line under the opt-out footer. The per-hauler drafts in
   `outreach/H1-H2_S1_Copy_v2.md` don't have it yet, and none can be sent until it's filled
   (CAN-SPAM; see README).

## How haulers win construction work (what the copy is built on)

- **The GC or construction manager is the buyer.** Waste guidance for construction projects
  puts one project authority, "usually the construction manager or general contractor", in
  charge of providing dumpsters and waste services for every trade (City of Lincoln). Texan Waste
  Services, in Houston, says it works "directly with contractors and project managers". The
  superintendent places and swaps containers day to day; the GC's office sets up the account.
- **It's decided early, and partly bundled.** Debris logistics are best planned "before work
  begins" as part of site logistics (Construction Review). Construction Business Owner tells
  contractors to have the dumpster partner lined up so equipment is "on-site and ready" when the
  crew is, and to bundle roll-offs with portable toilets to cut paperwork. Several of our haulers
  sell exactly that bundle (Rapid Waste, Moffitt, OP Enterprises, Z Sites).
- **Relationships win, lists open the door.** For subcontractors generally, "the highest-quality
  construction leads often don't come from any platform"; early lists mainly introduce you to a
  GC, and steady follow-up over six months to a year turns that into direct calls (ServiceTitan).
  This is why the sample's job is an introduction to the operator's office, not a promised job,
  and why the copy never implies a site is open.
- **What GCs complain about:** containers not on site when promised, poor service, messy
  invoicing (Dumpsters.com case study, a vendor's own material). Haulers lead with the opposite:
  same-day delivery, on-time swaps, one invoice. That's their pitch to the GC, and our fit line
  shouldn't compete with it. We only point at where to make it.
- **Timing caveat, from our own research:** the TCEQ record arrives around mobilization.
  Toilets are often placed then; most roll-off volume comes in vertical and finish-out
  (`docs/Business_Selection_Research.md`, section 2). The copy never says a hauler is or isn't
  in place, because no record shows either.

**What makes research read as credible rather than lead-gen spam**, applied here: name the
public record exactly so the reader can check it (TCEQ, TXR number, date); keep our opinion
visibly separate and labeled; say plainly that we have no tie to the project (and leave the
"how we work" detail to S2 and the sample, where it answers a question instead of padding S1); no hype words, no
urgency, no exclusivity (all banned in `ops/rules.json`); no links or attachments in a cold
email; one small ask. ASI's results support this: specific, checkable facts are what moved its
replies.

## S1 (business day 0)

**Subject (default, A):** `[Site name]`, plus `, [street]` only if the site name is under 10
characters.

**Template:**
```

TCEQ's Central Registry lists a construction stormwater authorization ([TXR15 number]) dated [Month Day] for [site name] at [street], [city] in [County] County. The operator on file is [operator business].

It's about [N] miles from your [yard city] yard, and [a/an] [commercial / industrial / construction] job like this is the kind your [published containers or services] serve; that's our read, not the filing.

We're a research service with no tie to the project. The sample costs nothing; after it, our 30-day pilot is a one-time $500 for 15–20 verified projects, with no subscription.

Want five sites like this near your yard, free?

Houston Jobsite Intelligence
Asterfield Group
sites@asterfieldgroup.com

If this isn't useful, reply "no thanks" and I won't email again.
[MAILING ADDRESS]
```

Rules for the slots: every value in the first paragraph comes from the TCEQ record, as filed
(recased for reading only). The distance is from the hauler's own published or FMCSA yard
address. The services come from the hauler's own website, never guessed. "Job" type is our read
of the site name; a person reviews it.

## The one A/B test (first 20 sends)

**Subject line only.** Everything else stays identical.

- **A:** `[Site name]` (as above). Specific and checkable.
- **B:** `Job site [N] miles from your yard`. Leads with what the hauler cares about, in its own
  words ("job site"), and lands at 30–33 characters.

Alternate A and B down each batch (row 1 A, row 2 B, and so on), so both arms go out on both
days. Log the arm with each send. With about 10 per arm, this can only show a large gap
(for example 3 replies against 0); treat anything smaller as a tie and keep A. Why this test:
Woodpecker's data points at subject length; it costs nothing to run; and it leaves the
one-fact formula intact so the main Tier B question is still measured cleanly.

## Objection replies

Each reply is short, answers from the record, and ends with the signature. Replies don't need the
opt-out footer, but never argue with a "no".

**"How did you get my email?"**
```
It's the address published on your website. We look up haulers near each new site from your own site and your FMCSA carrier record. Say the word and I won't email again.
```
(For B-dir grades, say "the address on your FMCSA carrier record".)

**"Has work started there? / Is it real?"**
```
The record shows an authorization date, not a start date. TCEQ's rules have the operator file before the site discharges stormwater, so it tells you the paperwork is in, nothing more. You can look it up yourself: [TCEQ record link]. The operator's office can tell you the schedule.
```

**"Does someone already haul there? / Is it open?"**
```
We can't tell you that, and nobody can from public records: no filing names the haulers on a site. That's why each site in the sample lists the operator's main office line; they're the ones to ask.
```

**"Who's the contact at the GC?"**
```
We list the operator's main business line only, never a person's name. It's a routing number, not a confirmed buyer; the front desk can point you to whoever handles site services on that job.
```

**"Can we get on a call?"**
```
I keep everything in email so it's all in writing, and I'm glad to answer anything here. What would you like to know?
```

**"What's the catch? / Is this a scam?"**
```
No catch on the sample: five sites, free, nothing to sign. Every fact in it links to the public TCEQ record so you can check it before you trust it. If it's useful, the 30-day pilot is a one-time $500 with no subscription; if not, that's the end of it.
```

**"How is this different from Dodge or ConstructConnect?"**
```
Those cover bids and planned projects across every trade, on a subscription. We do one thing: new state stormwater filings, matched to your yard and your containers, with the operator's office line on each. No subscription, and the sample is free so you can compare.
```

**"Do you send the same sites to my competitors?"**
```
A site can go to more than one hauler near it; we don't sell a site to just one. What we tailor is the list: the areas and kinds of jobs that fit your yard and containers.
```

**"Too expensive / just send more free ones."**
```
Fair question. The five-site sample is the free part; the pilot is $500 once for 15–20 sites over 30 days, and then it stops. If one job from the list isn't worth that to you, it isn't the right fit, and that's fine.
```

**"We have enough work." / "Not interested."**
```
Understood, thanks for letting me know. I won't email again.
```
(Log as a no; add to the do-not-contact file the same day, across both businesses.)

**"Remove me." / "no thanks"**: no reply needed beyond honoring it the same day. Add to the
do-not-contact file shared with ASI.

## Check before every send

- `python3 scripts/check_copy.py <file> --metro Houston --stage <stage>` prints PASS: price line
  exact (S1), signature, opt-out footer, banned phrases, no em dash.
- `[MAILING ADDRESS]` is filled in (the per-hauler drafts don't carry it yet).
- No person's name anywhere, including the site name and the greeting.
- The hauler isn't in ASI's lists or do-not-contact file; the site isn't in an ASI packet or sample.
- The services and yard in the fit line still match the hauler's own website today.
- The subject arm (A or B) is logged with the send.
